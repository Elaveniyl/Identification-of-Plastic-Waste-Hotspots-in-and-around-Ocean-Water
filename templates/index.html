System Architecture

Three Core Technical Pillars

The Stop Marine Pollution system integrates machine learning, geographic spatial analysis, and theoretical computer science to provide actionable intelligence on oceanic plastic waste.

1. AI Image Classification
Deep Learning Debris Analysis

Implements convolutional neural network inference using the Inception-v3 topology. The model evaluates marine debris images normalized to 299×299 RGB inputs across six distinct material categories: plastics, glass, metals, cardboard, paper, and general trash.

Explore Image Detection →
2. GIS Hotspot Prioritisation
Hotspot Prioritization Index (HPI)

Evaluates oceanic accumulation zones using geostatistical modeling across 3,532 survey coordinates. The composite index weights standardized mass concentration (50%), subtropical gyre containment (35%), and temporal recency (15%) to rank intervention priority.

Explore GIS Hotspots →
3. FLA Lifecycle Automata
Formal State Verification

Formally modeled as a Deterministic Finite Automaton (DFA) $M = (Q, \Sigma, \delta, q_0, F)$. Guarantees strict mathematical invariants across eight formal lifecycle states from draft field capture through geospatial tagging, operator review, and archive verification.

Environmental Context

Why Ocean Protection Matters

Marine ecosystems produce over half of Earth's atmospheric oxygen, absorb nearly a third of anthropogenic carbon dioxide emissions, and support essential food webs worldwide. An estimated 8 million metric tons of plastics enter waterways annually, fragmenting into durable microplastics that threaten marine biology.

By combining algorithmic debris recognition with quantitative hotspot prioritization, researchers and mitigation programs can target cleanup operations where plastic mass density causes the greatest ecological impact.

Atmospheric Oxygen

Phytoplankton and oceanic photosynthesis generate an estimated 50–80% of Earth's oxygen supply, sustaining planetary respiratory cycles.

Climate Regulation

The world ocean absorbs over 90% of excess heat trapped by greenhouse gases and approximately 30% of anthropogenic carbon dioxide emissions.

Marine Biodiversity

Over two million marine species inhabit ocean ecosystems, providing critical food security and vital biochemical compounds for modern medicine.
